# Edge cases: games that are not a 3D shooter

Each row is a recommended approach. Platform rules and tools change; check the current rules before you render.

| Case | Approach |
|---|---|
| **No characters; abstract or puzzle** | The mechanic is the hero. Tell-show-repeat (a card, then the footage that proves it) with 2-3 short cards; average shot 2.5-4.5 s (Baba Is You's trailer runs 4.25 s); macro camera and slow orbits on the board; the "aha" moment is the drop; type and music carry the emotion; no voice. The "receiving side" of a payoff is the consequence seen up close (a cascade, a collapse). Depth of field is safer here than anywhere, because there are no faces to lose. Vary scale (macro, mid, top-down) and colour per section, or every board looks alike. |
| **2D or pixel art** | Capture at native resolution with whole-pixel camera positions; upscale by an integer factor with nearest-neighbour (320x180 ×12, 384x216 ×10, 480x270 ×8, 640x360 ×6) with `scale=iw*8:ih*8:flags=neighbor`; zoom in integer steps or in the engine, never with smooth scaling in the edit; keep the art's own animation frame rate inside a 60 fps master (a 12 fps walk at 0.25x shows each drawing for 20 output frames, which is honest; never interpolate drawings); keep sprites on the pixel grid at every simulation step or slow motion shimmers; separate parallax layers give 2D its camera move; screen shake and palette flashes are the game's own grammar, kept where they mark the event; upload 4K even for 1080p viewers. |
| **Closed build: no source, no free camera** | Screen-capture at the highest resolution the build runs, at a constant frame rate with near-lossless settings (for example OBS at CQP 12-16, a constant 60 fps or higher); look for photo mode, replay or spectator modes, console commands and config switches for field of view and HUD; fake camera moves only as slow 5-10 % pushes on 4K captures, never upscaled beyond 1.25x; slow motion by running the game at 0.25x and recording in real time (single-player only) or by capturing at a high frame rate and laying it at 60 (the slow-motion budget is capture fps ÷ 60); say plainly what the limits are and lean harder on editing, music, sound and type. |
| **Unfinished or buggy** | Freeze and tag one build; plan around the known bugs in the game bible's risk list; restart the scene per take; frame out artefacts; label "work in progress" where true; never show features that will not ship. |
| **Multiplayer only** | Bots or scripted headless clients for crowds; 2-6 trusted players on voice with a shot director; a spectator or replay client for cameras; a private server, a fixed seed and map; capture each player's view. |
| **Procedural or random** | Expose seed control; log seeds per take; re-roll until the shot matches the brief. |
| **Very low-fidelity art** | Lean on editing, music, sound and type built from the game's own logo and palette; parallax key art; keep it 30-60 s. |
| **Mobile portrait** | Master at 1080x1920 (App Store previews at 886x1920, 30 fps); make 16:9 versions with designed side panels, never black bars; no hands or devices on screen. |
| **VR** | A stabilised spectator camera (60-75° field of view, smoothed), a third-person avatar flown by a virtual camera, or a mixed-reality composite; "Captured on [device]"; fill 16:9. Bell-shaped hand paths, never a perfectly still controller. |
| **Text-heavy (visual novel, narrative)** | Build around the 4-8 strongest lines as voice or kinetic type; show the UI briefly so the genre is honest; the story-trailer structure; hold text at least 0.3 s per word plus 1 s. |
| **No voice budget or no cast** | Kinetic type and in-game barks; synthetic voice only when it fits the world and the person approves the spend; otherwise no voice. Ask first whether it needs voice at all. |
| **The game's voice is subtitle-only** | Voice the lines with the game's own casting settings (OG Clash's cost about $0.007 a line, on an account the owner approved) or go without; never invent a narrator. |
| **15 s social cut** | The first frame is the hook; burned-in captions; on a 1080x1920 frame keep text out of the top 220-240 px, the bottom 480-660 px, the right 140 px and the side 60-120 px (text inside the central 880x1220 or so); check each app before posting. |
| **Localisation and subtitles** | Make the text-free master first with cards as layers; deliver stems (music, effects, voice) and a music-and-effects mix; an .srt per language (block captions with sound cues in brackets); leave 15-30 % slack for longer languages. |
| **Photosensitivity** | WCAG 2.3.1: no more than 3 general or red flashes in any 1 s over 25 % of a 10° field. Run an open-source analyser (EA's IRIS) on every cut; use a certified test for broadcast or paid ads; remove editorial white flashes first; add a warning card if strobes must stay. |
| **Rating and age** | No blood in TV, cinema and most paid online video ads; age-gate mature trailers; App Store previews are all-ages; make a clean cut beside the full one; put the regional rating block on the end slate where required (an icon at least 53 px high online). |
| **Platform logos and legal** | Official logo files only, under each holder's guidelines; no other platform's button glyphs; "© year Studio"; a music credit if the licence needs one; "Captured on [device]" and "Footage not final" where true. |
| **Other studios' work** | Never recreate another studio's game, characters, logo or footage, even renamed. No other games' names in voice lines (a line that was another game's title was cut from OG Clash), no other franchises' power-up names, and scan every reference-inspired frame for emblems (a period map once hid an offensive emblem on a tower). References are for measuring only. |
| **The game keeps changing during production** | Freeze; re-check the frozen build's receipt; decide per shot whether a newer build is visibly better; the trailer may lag the live game (OG Clash shipped 80 rounds behind the live build). |
| **The machine is shared with the game team** | Agree a split before production, run heavy jobs through a slot queue with thread caps, declare burst windows for final renders, yield new jobs to the game's profiling windows and never kill in-flight takes. The film does not need a quiet machine; the game's measurements do. |
| **No GPU, or a software renderer only** | Stepped capture needs a real GPU. Refuse software renderers (SwiftShader, llvmpipe): if no GPU exists, say so and stop rather than render footage that misrepresents the game. |
