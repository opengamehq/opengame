# The owner's eye: how a demanding owner reviews a game film

"The owner" is the person the film is for: the developer, the studio lead, whoever approves the cut. This chapter is
built from about 100 notes one demanding owner gave on the OG Clash launch trailer and 124 messages on the OG Snipers
montage, with the rule each note produced. Your owner is not this owner, but the shape repeats: owners judge what
they see on the newest file, they notice cheapness before craft, and they expect you to find what they would find.
Quotes are the owner's own words, typos kept, because the exact wording is how you learn to read a note.

The one sentence behind all of it: "i do NOT want to have to keep watching shitty takes and giving u feedbaxk thqt u
should be able to know ill give u". Anticipate. Never send a take they would reject.

## A. How they review

- They scrub the newest cut in a player, sometimes on a phone, and send **a crop of one frame with one blunt line**,
  often several within a minute. Treat a burst as one batch and say which cut it lands in.
- They name a shot by timecode ("at 0:07"). Put the frame number and bar.beat on every preview so notes come back
  frame-addressable.
- They watch the master too, and still find things after approval.
- Big structural notes come early. Once a direction is picked, notes get small: sizes, levels, "a tad".
- **They hedge and mean it.** "idk i may be wrong but just check that too" was right every time. Check a hedged
  question on frames and sweep the class.
- **They read "no X" literally and narrowly.** "no shimmer" meant no shimmer, not no lightning. Never widen a note.
- They freeze scope explicitly, then hand over the last mile: "make sure it looks sick and perfect and has nothing i
  wont like - im not going to respond now until you send me the 4k full cut".
- Voice dictation garbles names. Confirm any name or word that will appear in the film in one line.
- They want to **see** things: previews, the evolution, standalone renders of special elements, alternatives side by
  side. A file path is not delivery.
- On a montage they came back to the newest cut, not to every cut: notes on 7 of 19 numbered cuts, silent through
  twelve, then one note redirected a day of work. Fewer complete cuts with a change list serve them as well.
- About one message in five asks for a cut or for speed: "can i see latest cut btw", "its been a long time...",
  "how do we speed up". The worst case is a stall they learn about by asking: "please continue!" after 7 hours,
  "Hello?" after 3.5.
- They answer pictures and yes/no fast ("sure", "yea these ones are wrong") and leave open-ended sheets unanswered.
  Ask "which of these", with a default.
- They judge what they see: placeholders labelled as placeholders were reviewed as shots.
- They hear what gates miss: "some of hte gun sounds were way louder than others" while a stem gate read 0.1 dB.
- They may reverse within minutes ("lets remove the text", then sixteen minutes later "i liked the text and effect
  of from r17 so lets keep that"). Keep the last approved version until they have seen both.
- They expect the slop found for them: "im not going to call out every single slop point u need to find all this
  urself".
- They end a loop that drags ("yea lets call r19 final as is"). "Final candidate" with work still going into it read
  as not final.

## B. Principles every note boils down to

1. **Flow and aura over novelty.** "the whole thing needs to have FLOW and aura and clean smooth transitions and flow
   nothing jerky or sudden". Every shot has a reason to arrive; nothing random.
2. **Performed on the beat, not just cut on the beat.** "Everything in video needs to be perfectly synced w the music
   and micro polish macro polish at the deepest levels". The action inside the shot hits the beat.
3. **Speed ramps are the signature,** and they must be seen: real slow motion with motion through it.
4. **Nothing cheap.** A cheap effect is the film's defect; fix it at the source. The cleanest fix is often removal:
   "we dont need to show anything crazy ... beyond its existing visor".
5. **Nothing still that should move.** No standing characters, no static first person, no locked-off open, no freezes
   in slow motion.
6. **Only show what the engine does well.** Frame out puppets, feet, holds.
7. **Everything sharp and readable.** No depth-of-field mush; a new mechanic held long enough to digest; space between
   hero and enemies; geography that makes sense.
8. **One world.** The title and every insert lit, graded and sized like part of the world; every shot of a subject
   matches the shot they like.
9. **Characters talk; nobody announces.** Lines land where the speaker is on screen.
10. **Sound never cuts out,** never sounds cheap, and the music is always there.
11. **Production grade.** "how we show and present everything needs to be game of the year". The bar is the genre's
    newest official trailers and its best community editor. The shipped OG Clash trailer was rated 6/10 by its owner,
    two days after it shipped; aim for 11.
12. **Anticipate.** Never send a take they would reject.
13. **Speed and visibility.** Show progress constantly; never make them wait on a finished cut; give ETAs that hold.

What a montage added:

14. **Their taste outranks the reference they gave.** "its a constant beat so the sync and gun shots should be equal
    spacing and always on same beats" (the reference varies its gaps); "u cant have music just go completely blank"
    (the kit asked for silences); "we also shoudl let the full gun sounds play / echo" (a critic asked to trim them).
15. **The music leads, and the song is never edited.** "music is way too soft"; "gun sounds are way too loud relative
    to music"; "dont randomly change the song at 1:30?".
16. **Praise is a lock.** A defect note on a liked shot means repair that shot: "i liked that cool cinematic ... it
    just wasnt connected to body". Cutting the faulted shots made "cinematics got worse and flow/transitions got worse".
17. **Cinematics are about the place and the payoff, tied to the gameplay.** "i liked the [map] cinematic where the
    gun then transitioned in from a map zoom ... not all about just a single soldier"; "its not even the right
    person/bot or location of the last person to get sniped". They never praised the hero alone.
18. **Frame the impact on what animates well:** "show it at an angle where u dont see the bottom half of body".
19. **Real skilled play against real-looking opponents:** "never miss any shots and dont move unnaturally".
20. **Eased speed everywhere:** "smooth speed ramp transitions and even for cinematics speed ramps not just a constant
    speed of cinematics with awesome easing".
21. **Nothing passes through anything, nothing flickers, slow motion is smooth:** "we cant have z fighting/z
    flickering on the maps of any clips"; "super smooth slow mo no lag or frame jitter".
22. **Any game UI is the game's, pixel for pixel:** "why is there so much space after OG and the sniper icon? thats
    not how our kill feed looks".
23. **They decide the hero and the look of everything worn:** "this is a terrible camo to use". An audit's "usable"
    is not their "sick".
24. **One known folder for every cut:** "where are we saving all the cuts btw? what filepath".

## C. Anticipations: what they will flag next, as checks

Tick every row before they see anything. The thresholds and how to measure them are in the gates and measuring
chapters.

| They will say | Check |
|---|---|
| "this scene randomly comes in" | motion carried across the cut into the next shot's direction, or a stated cause |
| "character isnt moving at all ... boring" | camera ≥ 1 px/f every frame; first-person locomotion or aim motion |
| "just standing" / "unnatural with their hands out" | a pose sheet of every character frame; animation-audit verdicts |
| "looks terrible how zombie is animated" | no clip graded NO on screen; enemies in motion, at distance, in numbers |
| "looks really cheap" (an effect) | flat white ≤ 0.5 % on held hero effects; thin structured cores; no glow balls |
| "this depth of field blur looks terrible" | sharpness within the film's norm; no defocused occluder over 10 % of the frame |
| "coming from same side" | the screen-direction map honoured at every cut between sides |
| "so quick ... can barely digest" | a new mechanic held 70-90 frames at 60 fps |
| "not smooth ... sfx cut off" | no splice inside a hero action; tails across every cut |
| "freeze ... not a smooth flip" | angular rate ≥ 40 % of the median in every slow span |
| "it cant overlap with characters" / "doesnt look 3d" | title gap > 0 every frame; extrusion visible; matched to the plate |
| "no shimmer ... no particles" | the title is still at rest (bit-identical frames) |
| "not enough space" | clear space between hero and enemies |
| "feet glitch out" | no visible feet on crowd AI |
| "cheesy and terrible voice" | no announcer or narrator clichés; no other studio's names; recogniser-clean |
| "any voice should be said?" | each character's first appearance has a line or a reason |
| "where are we keeping track" / "can i see a preview" | a footage log, a review page, a preview every 30-60 minutes |
| "whats taking so long" | an ETA from measured throughput; never hold a finished cut |
| "the speed ramps??" | every hero action has a visible ramp with motion through it |
| "where's the story" | hook, want, turn, payoff and button in the plan and on screen |
| "off sync" / "equal spacing" (montage) | every payoff at beat − 3 frames, equal spacing, its sound attack within 10 ms of the beat |
| "music is way too soft" / "go completely blank" | the music leads; the song null-tests untouched; no hole |
| "some of the gun sounds were way louder" | per-payoff as-heard level on the delivered mix |
| "jittering" / "laggy" | every frame of every body in every slow span |
| "z flicker" | a flicker scan calibrated on their frame; places scouted at the planned lenses |
| "running endlessly up against a box" | every opponent followed through every frame they are visible |
| "goes through" | every outside frame at full size |
| "before you even shoot them" / "not the right person" | cause before effect; the cinematic's subject matches the payoff |
| "walks around and does nothing" / "looking left and right" | dead-time and searching checks between payoffs |
| "constant speed of cinematics" | ≤ 15 % speed change per frame; an eased V on every cinematic |
| "goes back to 100% too quick" | a zoom punch holds until the next payoff or cut |
| "cinematics got worse" | the praised list intact; an A/B against the last cut for anything lost |
| "wheres the final cut" | "final" only for what is final |

## D. The pre-send checklist

Tick PASS, FAIL or N/A with frames, the owner's own notes first:

1. Every gate passes, or its failure is listed as open in the message.
2. Every note from this round verified on the delivered render (frames and audio), not in code.
3. The cut carries the latest select for every shot whose fix you announced.
4. A blind critic and an owner proxy have seen it (or the message says they have not, and why).
5. A blind A/B against the previous cut found no shot worse, or the worse ones were reverted.
6. You watched and listened, start to finish at 1x (by measurement where you cannot listen), then strips across every
   seam and slow span.
7. The file plays inline, the link loads (you opened it yourself), and the message says what changed, what is open,
   where it is and the next ETA.
8. No slate, placeholder or proxy in a cut sent as a full cut, and no note they already gave left unfixed without
   saying so first.

## E. Recurring notes and the rule each produced

Each row is a note an owner gave, then the rule that would have prevented it. A note that comes twice means the
process did not learn; add the check that would have caught it.

| Note (verbatim, shortened) | Rule |
|---|---|
| "take the latest stable version and freeze that version for this video" | Freeze a named, hashed build for the film; the game keeps shipping past it. Play the frozen build before planning on it. |
| "Dolly cameras speed ramps sick cinematics slow motion ... fast to slow mo to fast" | Ramp every hero action: fast, slow on the payoff, back to fast. |
| "the cuts are on the beat but nothing actually happens on the beat" (a critic) | Sync the action inside the shot, not just the cut. |
| "40% first person is also fine btw" / "Or 30% whatever" | A first-person share is a band, not a target; measure it on the real cut and never reshoot to chase it. |
| "get rid of "the anvil wakes" line ... thats cheesy and terrible voice" | No announcer or voice-of-god lines. One cheesy line condemns its whole voice type. |
| "this scene look sterrible how zombie si aniamted" | Audit every animation on frames before planning a shot of it. |
| "the electricity here looks really cheap" | Fix a cheap effect at the source and sweep its whole class; no glow clipped to flat white. |
| "this scene randomly comes in and looks pretty bad" | Every shot needs a reason to arrive: a motion or cause handed over from the previous one. |
| "the character isnt moving at all and its just boring" | First person never stands still. |
| "this depth of field blur looks terrible here" | Every frame has a pin-sharp subject. And never paraphrase the owner into a stronger instruction: their "sick angle and depth of field" became a brief for "shallow DOF, f/2.8, soft foreground". |
| "the 3 ogs walking ... coming from same side a szombies ... idk i may be wrong" | Opposing sides enter from opposite sides and converge; map the level from above before choosing lenses. |
| "so quick and u can barely digest whats happened" | A shot that introduces a mechanic gets 1.2-1.5 s and one clear cause and result. 17 and 34 frames both failed; 86 frames held. |
| "this glow on the titans face ... is pretty cheap", then "we dont need to show anything crazy ... beyond its existing visor" | When something is called cheap, try removing it first. A "premium" replacement effect was rejected. |
| "how it appears those particles is terrible it should probably just be there... and no shimmer" | The title is simply there; the camera finds it. Still at rest. |
| "well crackling lightning is good with sick sfx etc... but no particles" | Read negatives narrowly: live structured energy on the beats was wanted; only particles and shimmer were out. |
| "it cant ovelrap with characters ... perfectly motinon tracked and angled", "not just an iamge thats motion tracked" | A 3D title is real geometry lit by the scene, sized like an object at its distance, never overlapping a character. |
| "the zombies hit the player and theres not enoguh space ... i liked the old scene" | Leave readable space; keep every rejected version restorable so a revert is one line. |
| "the camera moves but their animatiosn fo flipping ... freeze" | In slow motion nothing freezes: turn hitstop off and re-time short animations to span the slow window. |
| "the titans visor here is perfect thats the effect we need in that last scene" | When they point at a frame they like, make every other shot of that subject match it. |
| "What r u waiting on? Can u work faster?" (while a finished cut was held back) | Never hold a finished, verified cut while they wait. |
| "dont see anything on http://localhost:..." | Never send a link you have not loaded yourself. |
| "why werent u hitting and shooting its weak spots? Ur shooting the visor when thats not the objective" | Play the game correctly on camera: every beat fulfils its on-screen objective with visible success. |
| "i'd give that a 6/10 -> ideally this skill produces a 11/10" | The last film is the floor, not the target. |
| "are u sure we did the skills?" (on trailers made with a trimmed process) | Run plan, make and review-and-improve on every film, the last one repeated to its bar. They can tell when it was skipped. |
| "this is a terrible camo to use" | Ask for the owner's shortlist of skins and outfits; an audit's taste is not theirs. |
| "no dont change first person shots" | Changing the outside hero does not touch first-person shots. Never widen a note. |
| "we should have clips working bolt too ... that should only be a few clipsnof swapping" (answering a report that their own play swaps weapons after nearly every shot) | Never read a measured habit of the owner's as a rule for the film without asking what they want on screen. |
| "S dont sweat that part too much or nake it a hard rule" (one minute after a note read as "no name twice") | Read "never" or "all" in a quick aside as the direction, and check before turning it into a hard rule. |
| a shot ruled in from small ungraded thumbnails showed a head out of frame and a clipped sky once graded | Rule a shot on three or more full-size frames after the finishing pass; strips prove motion, not framing or exposure. Give the look a highlight guard for bright outside shots before locking it. |
| a shooter flagged the same continuity break twice and the director passed it | When someone flags a break twice, it is a break. |
| "gun shots are off sync ... equal spacing and always on same beats" | On a steady beat, payoffs at equal spacing on the same beats. |
| "u cant have music just go completely blank" | On the owner's song, no designed silences. |
| "there's a cinematic of them getting shot before you even shoot them" | Cause before effect, once. |
| "your right arm is just floating and there's no body" | An outside camera never shows the first-person rig. |
| "the bots legs go through the stairs", "the gun is sticking thorugh the body" | Nothing passes through anything; look at every outside frame at full size. |
| "the bot is just running endlessly up against a box" | Opponents behave like players in every frame they are visible. |
| "theres way too much z flickering on this map" (the film's own flicker gate had passed it) | Calibrate gates on the owner's flagged frames; change the framing or the place. |
| "every beat doesnt have to be a shot -> it could be movement ... or a new cinematic" | A grid point can carry a movement accent or a cut instead of a payoff. |
| "the first clips shouldnt be so many kills ... 5-6 pieces be the default ... last clip of 10 piece should be unique" | Escalation needs a floor: a default clip, a bigger one, a unique finale. |
| "so many pieces are still not fixed" | Do not send a cut with their notes still unfixed in it; say what is missing before rendering and let them choose. |
| "can we also add some other parts of kaisers editing ... nothing cheesy or chanign color scheme too much" | Ask about the reference editor's finishing layer with a short look test; lock the approved look. |
| "how can u kill 2 diff ubots in the video and they ahve the same usernaem?" | HUD shown in a film matches the shipped game pixel for pixel, with unique names from the game's own generator. |
| "cinematics and cut sequences btwn are much worse in latest version" | A shot they liked is fixed, never cut. A filler tied to nothing is worse than the flawed shot it replaced. |
| "arent the right person or location of who got sniped" | A payoff cinematic shows the right subject at the right place, from the same run. Never send placeholders. |
| "it should be floating in the air not on the ground" | A title floats in the scene, lit by it, reflecting it; letters come together from close by. |
| "Death cinematics at 31s, 34s, 41s, 50s, 61s, 70s, is bad" / "dont see the bottom half of body" | Decide the impact framing before tuning any animation. A day of fall tuning was made moot by this one note. |
| "Killionaire VoiceOver should start sooner right when u get the 10th kill" | A callout starts on its trigger; do not trade timing for intelligibility without asking. |
| "lets remove the text / lighting etc from the last scene... so no text" | When an element takes many rounds and is still not loved, offer the cut without it early. |
| "can we get the final cut asap?... taking too long" / "wheres the final cut... how do we speed up" | Answer with a measured bottleneck table and a scope cut in the same reply; call final only what is final. |

## Vocabulary

- **Good:** "sick", "aura", "flow", "micro polish / macro polish", "production grade", "smooth", "game of the year",
  "jawdropping", "sick angle", "well synced", "looks great".
- **Bad:** "random", "randomly comes in", "terrible", "cheap", "boring", "not smooth", "freeze", "cut off", "cheesy",
  "unnatural", "glitch out", "too big", "doesnt look 3d", "slop", "jerky", "jittering", "z flicker", "laggy", "make no
  sense", "weirdly cut", "floating", "sticking through", "ai slop".
- **Impatience:** "work faster", "asap", "whats taking so long", "how much longer?", "waiting on what?", "its been a
  long time...". Each followed a silent stall, a restart or a missed ETA.
